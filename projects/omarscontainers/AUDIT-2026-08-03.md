## 2. Blockers — submission is impossible until these clear

### B1. No payment method enabled — checkout cannot complete
`bacs`, `cheque`, `cod` all disabled. **Zero** enabled gateways. A customer cannot buy
anything. This is an automatic failure condition and also fails Google's "professional,
operational store" bar outright.

### B2. No shipping configured
Only the fallback zone ("Locations not covered by your other zones") exists, with
`ship_to_countries` empty and `allowed_countries = all` — the store currently offers
worldwide sale with no rates. For 2–8 tonne containers and trailers, shipping is a material
cost that cannot be left undefined.

### B3. No legal pages — and Germany is strict
Nothing exists at any German *or* English slug. Verified 404: `/impressum/`, `/datenschutz/`,
`/agb/`, `/widerruf/`, `/versand/`, `/kontakt/`, `/ueber-uns/`.

German law makes several of these mandatory independently of Google:
- **Impressum** — legally required for commercial sites (§5 DDG/TMG). Absent.
- **Datenschutzerklärung** — required under GDPR. Exists only as an unpublished WordPress draft.
- **Widerrufsbelehrung** — 14-day right of withdrawal, mandatory for consumer distance selling. Absent.
- **AGB**, **Versandkosten**, **Zahlungsarten** — absent.

The two draft pages are stock WordPress/WooCommerce boilerplate. Publishing them unedited
would satisfy nothing and directly trip "legal pages contain unrelated template content".

### B4. Store address empty
`store_address`, `store_city`, `store_postcode` are all blank. Nothing can be consistent
with nothing — this blocks the Impressum, the LocalBusiness schema, and Merchant Center
business-information parity simultaneously.

### B5. Twelve duplicate products
Six SKUs each exist twice — same name, same price, same SKU, different post IDs. A
double-import.

## 3. High priority

### H1. VAT is switched off — ~~RESOLVED 2026-08-03~~

> **Correction (2026-08-03).** This finding originally claimed "89 of 116 descriptions
> reference MwSt/USt". That was wrong — a faulty regex matched `ust` inside ordinary German
> words (*Wärmeverlust*, *Qualitätsverlust*) and `inkl.` in phrases like *"inkl. Lieferung"*.
> Verified re-check: **zero** genuine VAT statements exist in the product text. The
> contradiction reported here did not exist.

`calc_taxes` was `no`. German price-indication law (PAngV) requires consumer prices to be
gross, stated VAT-inclusive, with shipping costs disclosed.

**Resolved.** Owner confirmed **B2C**. Configured `prices_include_tax = yes`,
`tax_display_shop/cart = incl`, 19% DE standard rate, suffix
"inkl. 19 % MwSt. zzgl. Versandkosten". Existing prices are treated as **gross**, so
displayed prices are unchanged for customers (€4,660 remains €4,660 = €3,915.97 net +
€744.03 VAT).

### H2. No product attributes at all — 116/116
No brand, no condition, no structured specifications. Two consequences:

**Brand is recoverable from real data, not invented.** The catalogue is largely branded
goods — *Daikin*, *Cheval Liberté*, *Stahlworks* appear in product names. Those can be
extracted factually. I will not guess a brand for any product where one isn't evidenced.

**Branded goods raise the GTIN question.** For manufacturer-branded items Google expects a
GTIN, and its absence commonly limits performance or triggers disapproval. Daikin air
conditioners and Cheval Liberté trailers **have** EANs. This needs real numbers from
supplier documentation — never fabricated. Custom-built containers legitimately have none
and should use `identifier_exists: no`.

### H3. Product schema declares no itemCondition
Sample product page emits Product schema without `itemCondition`. Needs to be explicit and
match the feed — especially as new/used status is currently undeclared anywhere.

### H4. No contact route
No email or phone visible on the homepage, no contact page. Fails Google's transparency
guideline and Germany's Impressum requirement together.

### H5. Catalogue coherence
The catalogue spans containers, air conditioners, horse trailers, boat trailers, pool robots
and pool containers. That's a wide spread for a business named "Omars Containers", and
reviewers assess whether a store looks like a coherent operating business.
**Not a blocker** — but worth a deliberate answer in the About page and category structure.
