# Misrepresentation — the #1 suspension cause

Misrepresentation means the store, feed, or ads present the business or its
products in a way Google finds untrustworthy or deceptive. It is enforced
harder than almost anything else: **suspensions can happen with no warning**,
Google **won't tell you the specific cause**, and you get roughly **three
review attempts** before the "request review" option disappears. Since the
April 2026 AI-led review, a language model reads the crawler's rendered view of
each page and scores it — so everything below must be true on the *public,
rendered* site, not just in a CMS draft.

Google evaluates four pillars: **business identity**, **contact reachability**,
**complete & consistent policies**, and a **trustworthy checkout**. Fail any and
the whole account is at risk.

## 1. Business identity (must be real and consistent)

- A clear, real business/company name, presented the same way across the site,
  checkout/receipts, Merchant Center account, and domain registration.
- **No fabricated people.** An invented founder, team member, or biography is
  textbook false identity. If you name a founder, it must be a real person with
  a true history. Removing an invented persona is always a safe fix; inventing a
  real-sounding one is a violation.
- No fake or copied "About" story, no borrowed company history, no claimed
  awards/press/partnerships that didn't happen.
- No claim of "authorized dealer / official retailer / brand partner" unless a
  signed agreement exists. Independent resellers must say so plainly.
- Business identity in the feed/ads must match the destination site (the store
  the shopper lands on must be the store that's claimed).

## 2. Contact information (complete and reachable)

- At least two, ideally all three: **email**, **phone**, **physical address** —
  visible on the site (a `/contact` page and/or footer), not hidden.
- Details must be genuine and monitored. A dead phone number or unrouted email
  reads as a shell store.
- Keep contact details consistent everywhere they appear (site, policies,
  WHOIS, Merchant Center). Area code / country consistent with the stated
  address.

## 3. Policies (present, original, complete, consistent)

Every store needs these as real, dedicated pages — not a single vague blob:

- **Returns & refunds** — return window, who pays return shipping, restocking
  fees, refund method, and the **refund processing time** stated as a real
  number. This is Google's most-scrutinized policy.
- **Shipping** — regions served, methods, costs (or clearly "free"), and
  delivery estimates. Must match what checkout actually charges.
- **Privacy policy** — what data is collected and how it's used; must reflect
  what the site actually does (analytics, marketing pixels, etc.).
- **Terms & conditions** — including governing law/jurisdiction tied to the real
  business location.
- Optional but trust-building: warranty, payment methods, cookie policy, FAQ.

Policy rules that trip stores up:
- **No placeholder leaks.** Bracketed `[confirm: …]`, `TBD`, `Lorem ipsum`,
  empty blanks (`** business days**`), or template instructions left in the
  published text are instant credibility failures — and a language-model
  reviewer spots them immediately.
- **Internal consistency.** The returns window on the product page, the returns
  policy page, and the Merchant Center feed's return settings must all agree. A
  "free shipping" banner that becomes a shipping charge at checkout is a classic
  contradiction Google flags.
- Policies must be **original** (not copy-pasted from another store) and must
  describe *your* actual operations.

## 4. Checkout & trust

- Every advertised product must be **actually purchasable** — working
  add-to-cart and a completable checkout, no dead ends.
- HTTPS across the whole site, especially cart/checkout. No mixed-content or
  certificate warnings.
- Clear, honest pricing: the price in the feed/ad = the price on the landing
  page = the price at checkout. No bait pricing, no surprise fees, no "too good
  to be true" deals with no explanation.
- A clear charge/billing descriptor so the shopper recognizes the charge.
- No aggressive pop-ups or interstitials that block the crawler or the shopper
  from reaching product and checkout.

## The misrepresentation checklist (run before every submit/appeal)

Google's 2026 review effectively scores dozens of factors across these buckets.
Confirm each is TRUE:

**Identity**
- [ ] Real, consistent business name everywhere.
- [ ] No fabricated founder/team/awards/press/partnerships.
- [ ] "Authorized dealer" claims only if genuinely authorized.
- [ ] Domain registrant / Merchant Center / checkout name all coherent.

**Contact**
- [ ] Email + phone + address visible and genuine.
- [ ] All contact channels monitored and consistent.

**Policies**
- [ ] Returns, shipping, privacy, terms all exist as real pages.
- [ ] Refund window/processing time stated with real numbers.
- [ ] Zero placeholder/bracket/TBD/blank text on any published page.
- [ ] Returns/shipping match product pages AND the feed's return/shipping data.
- [ ] "Free shipping" (if claimed) is actually free at checkout.
- [ ] Policies are original and describe real operations.

**Checkout & pricing**
- [ ] Every advertised product is purchasable.
- [ ] Feed price = landing-page price = checkout price.
- [ ] HTTPS everywhere; no cert/mixed-content warnings.
- [ ] No blocking pop-ups; crawler can reach products and checkout.

**Product truthfulness**
- [ ] Titles/descriptions/images accurately describe the actual item.
- [ ] No inflated/fake "was" prices or countdowns that don't reset honestly.
- [ ] Reviews/testimonials are real and verifiable (or hidden until they exist).

If any box can't be truthfully checked, it's an owner action — get the real
fact, don't invent one.
