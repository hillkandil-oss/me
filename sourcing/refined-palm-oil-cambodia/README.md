## 1. The honest framing on "best price in the market"

Palm oil is not a product where a supplier has a secret cheap source. It is a **globally traded
commodity quoted daily**, with published FOB benchmarks that every serious counterparty in the chain
can see. A trader quoting 10% under the market is either not delivering, delivering off-spec, or
about to re-trade you when the market moves.

So the offer does not try to win on a headline number. It wins on the four things that actually
change a Cambodian buyer's landed cost:

| Lever | Worth |
|---|---|
| **ATIGA Form D certificate of origin** | **~$1,900 per 22 MT container** vs the 7% MFN band |
| Correct packing choice (bulk vs packed) | $55–265/MT — the biggest single spread in the offer |
| Price validity discipline (48 h, not 30 days) | protects both sides in a market that moved 2%+ in a fortnight |
| Grade selection (CP10 vs CP8, fortified or not) | $8–15/MT |

The Form D point is the one to lead with. It is a real, quantifiable saving, it is documentary rather
than negotiable, and a competing offer that omits it is not comparable on landed cost.

## 4. Cambodian import mechanics

- **HS code:** **1511.90** covers refined/RBD palm oil and its fractions including olein and stearin.
  (1511.10 is crude palm oil.)
- **Duty bands:** Cambodia applies ad valorem rates of **0%, 7%, 15% and 35%**.
- **ATIGA preference:** ASEAN-origin goods qualify for **0–5%** with a **Certificate of Origin
  Form D** and satisfaction of the ASEAN Rules of Origin (the general test is 40% ASEAN content;
  refined palm oil from Malaysia or Indonesia is wholly obtained within ASEAN and clears this
  comfortably).
- **VAT:** flat **10%**, applied to CIF + duty + any special taxes.
- ⚠️ **Verify the exact line.** I could not confirm the specific ATIGA rate for 1511.90 into Cambodia
  from a primary source. The offer therefore states the saving as an illustration and asks the buyer
  to confirm the band with the **General Department of Customs and Excise**. Do not put an
  unverified duty rate in writing as a guarantee.

**Trade context:** Cambodia's vegetable and palm oil imports come predominantly from **Malaysia
(~39%)** and **Indonesia (~35%)**, with some Vietnamese re-export. Loading Port Klang or Pasir
Gudang puts you on the main established lane.

## 5. Product and market-fit notes

- **CP10 vs CP8** — cloud point 10 °C vs 8 °C max. CP8 is more cold-stable and clearer; small premium.
  For Cambodia's climate CP10 is normally sufficient for general cooking-oil use.
- **Vitamin A fortification** — Cambodia has one of Southeast Asia's higher micronutrient-deficiency
  burdens and has pursued fortification of vegetable oil, fish sauce and soy sauce; the reference
  level for oil is **18 ppm vitamin A**. I could not confirm whether fortification is *mandatory* for
  imported oil, so the offer presents it as an option and asks the buyer to confirm their
  requirement. Worth raising — it is the kind of detail that decides a tender.
- **Halal** — JAKIM (Malaysia) or MUI (Indonesia) certification is standard on this origin and costs
  nothing extra to include. Include it by default.
- **Sustainability** — MSPO/ISPO are origin-country schemes; RSPO segregated or mass-balance carries
  a premium and matters only if the buyer's own customers demand it. Ask rather than assume.
